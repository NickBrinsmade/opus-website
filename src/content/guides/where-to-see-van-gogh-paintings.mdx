<span class="eyebrow">Quick answer</span>

**Go to the Netherlands:** the **Van Gogh Museum** in Amsterdam (more than 200 paintings) and the **Kröller-Müller Museum** in Otterlo (88, all on show together until **January 3, 2027**) hold about a third of everything he painted. **The single most famous work,** *The Starry Night*, is at **MoMA** in New York. **Paris** (Musée d’Orsay) and **London** (National Gallery, Courtauld) have the best of the rest.

## Why the Netherlands has so many

Van Gogh sold almost nothing in his lifetime, so when he died in 1890 most of his work belonged to his brother Theo, who died six months later. Theo’s widow, **Jo van Gogh-Bonger**, spent the rest of her life promoting the paintings and publishing the letters. Her family’s collection became the Van Gogh Museum, which opened in 1973.

The other great collection was bought. Between 1908 and 1929 **Helene Kröller-Müller**, one of the first serious Van Gogh collectors, bought around 90 of his paintings and more than 180 works on paper. They are now the Kröller-Müller Museum, which she built in the middle of a national park.

## The best Van Gogh trips

**Amsterdam and Otterlo, this winter.** Spend a morning at the Van Gogh Museum, then make the trip to the Kröller-Müller. Until January 3, 2027, its exhibition *Van Gogh, All Our Paintings* shows all 88 of its Van Goghs at once, for the first time since 1984. It’s inside De Hoge Veluwe National Park, so allow a full day and pay attention to the park’s opening hours as well as the museum’s.

**Paris, plus Auvers.** The Orsay has the late self-portrait, *The Church at Auvers* and *Starry Night Over the Rhône*. Then take the train to **Auvers-sur-Oise**, where he spent his last 70 days. You can see the church he painted and the wheat fields, and visit his grave beside Theo’s. There are no paintings in Auvers, just the places in them.

**New York in a day.** *The Starry Night* and *The Olive Trees* at MoMA, the Met’s Van Goghs, including *Wheat Field with Cypresses*, and two more at the Guggenheim.

**London, free.** The National Gallery’s *Sunflowers* and *Van Gogh’s Chair* cost nothing to see. The Courtauld, a short walk away, has the *Self-Portrait with Bandaged Ear*.

## The Van Goghs you can’t see

- **Sunflowers, destroyed.** One of the seven vases was in a private collection in Ashiya, Japan, and burned in an air raid in 1945.
- **Poppy Flowers, stolen.** Taken from the Mohamed Mahmoud Khalil Museum in Cairo in 2010 and never recovered.
- **The private ones.** The first *Portrait of Dr Gachet* sold at auction in 1990 for $82.5 million, then a record. It has rarely been seen since. One *Sunflowers* is also privately owned.

A happier footnote: two paintings stolen from the Van Gogh Museum in 2002 were found in Italy in 2016 and are back on its walls. *The Parsonage Garden at Nuenen*, stolen from a Dutch museum during the 2020 lockdown, was recovered in 2023.

## Before you go

- **Book the Van Gogh Museum in advance.** It sells timed tickets online only, and popular slots go days ahead.
- **Expect copies everywhere.** Van Gogh often painted several versions of a subject (*Sunflowers*, *The Bedroom*, *Wheat Field with Cypresses*, the Roulin family portraits), so the one you know may not be the one in front of you. Check the date on the label.
- **Russia’s Van Goghs** (the Pushkin in Moscow, the Hermitage in St Petersburg) include *The Red Vineyard*. Check current travel advice before planning a trip around them.
