**First floor:** the **Temple of Dendur** (Gallery 131) and the American Wing’s **Washington Crossing the Delaware** (760) and **Madame X** (771). **Second floor:** the **Vermeers** (614), **Rembrandt’s Aristotle** (616), **Bruegel’s Harvesters** (638), then **Van Gogh, Monet and Degas** in the 800s. **Allow three hours,** and go at opening or on a Friday or Saturday evening.

**How this list works.** We chose these works ourselves: the ones most people come for, plus a handful of quieter favorites. Then we checked each one’s gallery number in the Met’s own collection data on November 16, 2026. Works move for loans and rehangs, so if a gallery number doesn’t match, ask at the information desk.

## The route

Enter through the Great Hall on Fifth Avenue. The route runs from the first floor up to the second, and takes about three hours without rushing.

### First floor: Egypt, Greece and America

1. **Mastaba Tomb of Perneb** (Gallery 100). A real Egyptian tomb chapel from about 2350 BC that you can walk into, right at the start of the Egyptian wing. Most people rush past it on the way to Dendur.
2. **The Temple of Dendur** (Gallery 131). A whole Egyptian temple, given to the US by Egypt in 1965 and rebuilt in a glass-walled hall beside Central Park. Go early, before the room fills.
3. **Marble statue of a kouros** (Gallery 154). One of the earliest Greek marble statues of a standing youth, from about 590–580 BC. Stiff, symmetrical and strangely modern.
4. **Canova’s Perseus and Carpeaux’s Ugolino** (Gallery 548). Two great sculptures in the European sculpture court: the calm, classical hero and a father starving in prison with his sons.
5. **The American Wing, Gallery 760.** *Washington Crossing the Delaware*, almost 4 by 6.5 meters, hangs here with Church’s *Heart of the Andes* and Bierstadt’s *The Rocky Mountains, Lander’s Peak*: three of the biggest showpieces of American painting in one room.
6. **Eakins and Homer** (Galleries 763 and 767). *The Champion Single Sculls (Max Schmitt in a Single Scull)* and Homer’s *The Gulf Stream*, a sailor adrift among sharks.
7. **Sargent’s Madame X** (Gallery 771). The portrait that caused a scandal at the 1884 Paris Salon, mainly over a fallen shoulder strap that Sargent later repainted. He kept it for 30 years, then sold it to the Met.

### Second floor: European paintings

Take the Great Hall stairs up to the European paintings galleries, the 600s.

8. **Titian’s Venus and Adonis** (Gallery 608).
9. **The Vermeers** (Gallery 614). All five of the Met’s Vermeers hang together, including *Young Woman with a Water Pitcher*. Nowhere else in the world has more in one room. ([Where to see every Vermeer.](/guides/where-to-see-vermeer-paintings/))
10. **Rembrandt’s Aristotle with a Bust of Homer** (Gallery 616). Bought in 1961 for $2.3 million, then the highest price ever paid for a painting at auction.
11. **Caravaggio’s The Musicians** (Gallery 620).
12. **Velázquez’s Juan de Pareja** (Gallery 625). A portrait of his assistant, an enslaved man whom Velázquez later freed and who became a painter himself.
13. **David’s The Death of Socrates** (Gallery 634).
14. **Duccio’s Madonna and Child** (Gallery 635). Small enough to hold, around 1290–1300, and one of the most expensive purchases in the Met’s history. You can still see the candle burns on the frame.
15. **Bruegel’s The Harvesters** (Gallery 638). Peasants eating lunch in a wheat field in 1565. Many visitors’ favorite painting in the building.

### Second floor: the 19th century and modern art

16. **Degas** (Galleries 815 and 816). *The Dance Class* (815) and his dancers in pastel and oil. Look for his sculpture *The Little Fourteen-Year-Old Dancer* in the Degas rooms.
17. **Monet** (Galleries 818 and 819). *Garden at Sainte-Adresse* and *Bridge over a Pond of Water Lilies*.
18. **Van Gogh** (Galleries 822 and 825). *Wheat Field with Cypresses* and *Self-Portrait with a Straw Hat*.
19. **Pollock’s Autumn Rhythm** (Gallery 899). More than 5 meters of poured paint.
20. **Picasso’s Gertrude Stein** (Gallery 965). Stein said she sat for it some 90 times, and Picasso finished the face from memory.

## Not on view right now

**El Greco’s View of Toledo** has no gallery listed in the Met’s collection data as of November 16, 2026, which usually means it is off view or on loan. If it’s the painting you came for, check its page on the Met’s website before you go.

## Tickets, hours and timing

- **Admission** is a fixed price for most visitors and includes special exhibitions and same-day entry to **The Met Cloisters**, the medieval branch in Fort Tryon Park. New York State residents and students from New York, New Jersey and Connecticut pay what they wish.
- **Hours:** the Met is closed on Wednesdays and open late on Fridays and Saturdays. Check the website for holiday changes.
- **Least crowded:** weekday mornings at opening, and Friday and Saturday evenings.
- **Rooftop:** in the warmer months the Cantor Roof Garden has a commissioned installation and the best view of Central Park from any museum.
- **Photos** are allowed for personal use, without flash. ([Photo rules at other museums.](/guides/can-you-take-photos-in-museums/))

## If you have a second day

Go uptown to **The Met Cloisters** (it needs its own ticket on a different day; same-day entry is included with Fifth Avenue) for the *Unicorn Tapestries* (Gallery 017) and medieval gardens above the Hudson. Or come back for the arms and armor court, the Astor Chinese garden court and the musical instruments, three of the most loved collections in the building.
