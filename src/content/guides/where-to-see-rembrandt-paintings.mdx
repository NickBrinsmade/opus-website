<span class="eyebrow">Quick answer</span>

**Amsterdam first:** the **Rijksmuseum** has the largest Rembrandt collection in the world, including *The Night Watch* (being restored in public view) and *The Jewish Bride*, and his house is a short tram ride away. **The Hague**’s Mauritshuis has *The Anatomy Lesson*. **London** has the most you can see free: the National Gallery, Kenwood House and the Wallace Collection. **New York** has the Met and the Frick.
</div>

**How this list works.** Rembrandt is the hardest painter in this series to pin down. Wikidata lists 859 paintings under his name, more than twice what scholars accept today, because it includes old attributions, workshop pieces and collections that dispersed long ago. We cross-checked those entries against our own museum data and kept the major works in public collections whose attribution is settled. Checked November 5, 2026.

## A Rembrandt day in Amsterdam

1. **The Rijksmuseum, at opening.** Go straight to the Gallery of Honour for *The Night Watch*. Since 2019 it has been the subject of **Operation Night Watch**, the largest research and restoration project in its history, carried out inside a glass chamber so visitors can watch. Then find *The Jewish Bride*, *The Syndics* and the late *Self-Portrait as the Apostle Paul*.
2. **The Rembrandt House Museum, afternoon.** A short tram ride (or a 30-minute walk) away is the house he bought in 1639 at the height of his success and lost in his 1656 bankruptcy. The rooms have been rebuilt from the inventory made when his possessions were sold, including his studio and his cabinet of curiosities. There are few paintings, but there are etching demonstrations on the press.
3. **The Hague, the next day.** The Mauritshuis is under an hour away by train: *The Anatomy Lesson of Dr Nicolaes Tulp*, painted when he was 26, and a self-portrait from 1669, the year he died. While you’re there, see [Vermeer’s Girl with a Pearl Earring](/guides/where-to-see-vermeer-paintings/).

## London’s free Rembrandts

London might be the best city in the world for seeing Rembrandt for nothing. The **National Gallery** has the self-portraits at 34 and at 63, a pair that shows the confident young success and the old man three decades later. **Kenwood House** on Hampstead Heath has the late *Self-Portrait with Two Circles*, one of his greatest. The **Wallace Collection** has his son *Titus*. All three are free.

## Rembrandt or not?

Rembrandt ran a busy workshop, and his pupils painted in his style, sometimes on his canvases. For more than 40 years from 1968, the **Rembrandt Research Project** examined every painting attributed to him, and it moved dozens in both directions.

The most famous casualty is in Berlin. *The Man with the Golden Helmet* was one of the most loved “Rembrandts” in the world until 1985, when the Gemäldegalerie reattributed it to an unknown artist in his circle. It still hangs there, and it’s still worth seeing. Other paintings have gone the other way: works long dismissed as followers’ have been restored to Rembrandt after technical study.

So if a label says “Rembrandt and workshop”, “circle of Rembrandt” or “attributed to”, that isn’t a hedge. It’s the current state of a century of research.

## The Rembrandt you can’t see

*The Storm on the Sea of Galilee* (1633), his only seascape, was cut from its frame at the Isabella Stewart Gardner Museum in Boston in March 1990, in the same robbery that took [Vermeer’s *The Concert*](/guides/where-to-see-vermeer-paintings/). It has never been recovered, and the empty frame still hangs in the Dutch Room, next to his self-portrait at 23, which the thieves left behind.
