<span class="eyebrow">Quick answer</span>

**Usually yes:** most major museums allow photos for personal use, **without flash, tripods or selfie sticks**. **The big exceptions:** the **Prado** (no photos in the galleries), the **Sistine Chapel** and the **Frick Collection**’s galleries. **Temporary exhibitions** often ban photos even where the permanent collection allows them, so look for signs at the entrance.
</div>

**How this list works.** We checked each museum’s rules on its own website or in recent reporting in November 2026, and we’ll update this page as policies change. Rules can differ by room and by exhibition, so a sign in the gallery always beats this list.

## Why some museums still say no

- **Crowds.** The Prado’s stated reason is the one many visitors privately agree with: allowing photos would turn its most famous rooms into queues for selfies with *Las Meninas*.
- **Lenders and copyright.** Temporary exhibitions borrow from other museums and collectors, whose loan agreements often forbid photography. Works by artists who died less than 70 years ago (in most of Europe and the US) may still be under copyright, so museums restrict photos of them.
- **Conservation.** This is why flash is banned almost everywhere. Light damage is cumulative, and works on paper, textiles and photographs are the most vulnerable.
- **Contemplation.** Some museums, like the Frick, ban photos in the galleries simply to keep them calm.
- **History.** The Sistine Chapel ban dates back to the restoration of the frescoes, which was funded by a Japanese broadcaster that received exclusive photo rights. Those rights expired long ago, but the ban stayed, now justified by the crowds.

## How to take good museum photos (without being that person)

1. **Turn off the flash before you go in.** On most phones, flash is set to auto. Switch it off once and forget it.
2. **Photograph the label too.** It’s the single most useful photo you’ll take: artist, title, date and accession number, so you can find the work again later.
3. **Shoot slightly off-axis.** Standing a little to one side avoids glare on glass and varnish. You can correct the angle afterwards.
4. **Don’t block the view.** Take your photo and step aside. If there’s a crowd, take one shot and come back.
5. **No selfie sticks or tripods.** They’re banned almost everywhere, for good reason.
6. **Look before you shoot.** It’s easy to spend a whole visit looking at paintings through a screen. Take the photo, then put the phone down and look.
