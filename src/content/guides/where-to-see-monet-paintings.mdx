<span class="eyebrow">Quick answer</span>

**Start in Paris:** the **Marmottan** (the largest Monet collection in the world, including *Impression, Sunrise*), the **Orsay**, and the **Orangerie**’s eight *Water Lilies* panels. This winter both the Orangerie (until **January 25**) and the Marmottan (until **January 31, 2027**) are also running Monet exhibitions. **In the US,** Chicago, Boston, New York and Washington all have major groups. **Giverny** is closed until spring.

## Where the series went

Monet often painted one subject again and again in different light, then sold the canvases separately. So each series is now spread across many museums:

- **Water Lilies** (about 250 canvases, 1897–1926): the Orangerie’s eight panels are the heart of it. Large late panels are at MoMA, the Marmottan, the National Gallery in London, Tokyo’s National Museum of Western Art and the Kunsthaus Zürich.
- **Haystacks** (25 canvases, 1890–91): the Art Institute of Chicago has the largest group in any museum. Others are at the Orsay, the Met, the MFA Boston and the Museum Barberini in Potsdam.
- **Rouen Cathedral** (more than 30 canvases, 1892–94): the Orsay hangs five together. Others are at the Met, the National Gallery of Art in Washington and Rouen’s own fine arts museum.
- **Houses of Parliament** (London, 1900–05): the Orsay and the National Gallery of Art each have one.
- **Gare Saint-Lazare** (1877): versions at the Orsay, the National Gallery in London and the Art Institute of Chicago.

## The best Monet trips

**Paris this winter.** 2026 marks 100 years since Monet died, and Paris is making the most of it. A full Monet day: the **Marmottan** in the morning for *Impression, Sunrise* and the late Giverny paintings, the **Orangerie** at midday for the *Water Lilies* and the centenary show *Monet, peindre le temps*, then the **Orsay** in the afternoon. Book the Orangerie ahead. ([See everything on in Paris.](/guides/best-art-exhibitions-paris/))

**Giverny, from April.** Monet’s house and gardens are about 75 km from Paris and open from **April 1 to November 1**. The gardens are the point: the water-lily pond and the Japanese bridge he painted for more than 20 years. The house holds his collection of Japanese prints, but no original paintings. The nearby **Musée des impressionnismes Giverny** has exhibitions.

**Normandy.** Monet grew up in **Le Havre**, where he painted *Impression, Sunrise*; its MuMa museum has Monets and a view of the same harbor. **Rouen**’s fine arts museum has one of the cathedral paintings, and the cathedral itself is a few minutes’ walk away.

**Berlin and Potsdam.** The **Museum Barberini**, 30 minutes from Berlin, shows about 40 Monets from the Hasso Plattner Collection, always on view. It’s the largest group in Europe outside Paris, and many travelers don’t know it exists.

**Chicago or Boston.** Both have among the largest Monet collections outside France. Chicago has the best group of Haystacks anywhere, and Boston has *La Japonaise*, his life-size portrait of his wife in a kimono.

## Coming to London

The Orangerie’s centenary exhibition moves to **Tate Modern from February 25 to June 27, 2027**. If you can’t get to Paris this winter, that’s the next chance to see a large group of late Monets together. ([More on London exhibitions.](/guides/best-art-exhibitions-london/))

## Before you go

- **Read the date on the label.** With so many versions of the same subjects, the Water Lilies or Haystack in front of you may be from a different year, and a very different mood, from the one you know.
- **Late Monet is big.** The late Water Lilies panels are meters wide and meant to surround you. Reproductions don’t prepare you for the scale, so give them time and sit down if there’s a bench.
- **Check loans.** Centenary exhibitions borrow heavily, so a Monet that normally hangs in one museum may be in another city this year.
