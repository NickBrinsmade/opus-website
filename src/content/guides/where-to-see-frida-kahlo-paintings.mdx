**Mexico City has the most:** the **Museo Dolores Olmedo** (the largest collection, reopened May 2026), *The Two Fridas* at the **Museo de Arte Moderno**, and her home, **La Casa Azul**. **In the US,** MoMA has three, and single works are in San Francisco, Washington, Buffalo, Phoenix and Austin. **In Europe this winter,** Tate Modern’s *Frida: The Making of an Icon* runs until **January 3, 2027**.

## Why her paintings are hard to see

Kahlo made about 200 paintings in a life cut short at 47, after a bus accident at 18 left her in pain for the rest of it. About a third are self-portraits. Most are small, many are privately owned, and her fame now means that the ones in museums travel constantly for exhibitions.

Two other things shape where you can see her. In **1984 Mexico declared her work a national artistic monument**, so paintings in Mexico can’t leave permanently. And for six years until 2026, the largest public collection was **closed**: the Museo Dolores Olmedo shut in 2020 and reopened only on May 30, 2026.

## Two days of Frida in Mexico City

**Day one: Coyoacán and San Ángel.** Start at **La Casa Azul**, the house in Coyoacán where she was born, lived with Diego Rivera and died in 1954. Book the earliest slot online, because the house is small and fills up. There are a few paintings, but the house is the point: her studio, her wheelchair at the easel, the mirror above her bed that she painted in, and her Tehuana dresses. Then go to the **Museo Casa Estudio Diego Rivera y Frida Kahlo** in San Ángel, the pair of houses, one blue and one red, joined by a bridge, where they lived and worked in the 1930s.

**Day two: Chapultepec and Xochimilco.** See *The Two Fridas*, painted the year she and Rivera divorced, at the **Museo de Arte Moderno** in Chapultepec Park. Then head south to the **Museo Dolores Olmedo** in Xochimilco for the largest collection of her work, including *The Broken Column* and *Henry Ford Hospital*. Olmedo was a friend and patron of Rivera, and the museum is in her 16th-century hacienda, where peacocks and the Mexican hairless dogs Kahlo loved roam the gardens.

## Frida in London this winter

**Frida: The Making of an Icon** at Tate Modern runs until **January 3, 2027**. It looks at how her image became one of the most recognizable in the world. It’s the easiest way to see a large group of her paintings outside Mexico this year. Book ahead, because it’s busy right up to the end. ## Before you go

- **Check loans first.** Kahlo’s museum paintings are in heavy demand for exhibitions, so the one you’re traveling for may be on another continent. Check its page, or ask the museum.
- **The Pompidou’s Kahlo is unavailable.** *The Frame*, the first work by a 20th-century Mexican artist bought by a major international museum, is in the Centre Pompidou’s collection, and the building is closed until 2030.
- **Expect merchandise.** Kahlo’s image is everywhere. The paintings themselves are small and intimate, so give them time.
