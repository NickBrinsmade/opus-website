<span class="eyebrow">Quick answer</span>

**Go to Vienna:** the **Upper Belvedere** (*The Kiss*, *Judith I* and the world’s largest Klimt collection), the **Beethoven Frieze** at the Secession, the **Leopold Museum** (*Death and Life*) and the early Klimts above the **Kunsthistorisches Museum**’s staircase. **In New York,** the Neue Galerie has the *Woman in Gold*. Single masterpieces are in **Prague**, **Rome**, **Venice**, **Ottawa** and **Washington**.

## A Klimt day in Vienna

You can see the whole arc of his career in a day, from academic beginnings to the golden period to the late, unfinished portraits.

1. **Kunsthistorisches Museum, morning.** On the main staircase, look up. Between the columns are paintings Klimt made in 1890–91, at 28, alongside his brother Ernst and Franz Matsch. They’re polished and historical, nothing like what came later, which is exactly why they’re worth seeing first.
2. **The Secession, late morning.** Ten minutes’ walk away is the gold-domed building Klimt helped found in 1897, with its motto “To every age its art, to art its freedom”. In the basement is the *Beethoven Frieze*, his monumental response to Beethoven’s Ninth Symphony.
3. **Leopold Museum, lunchtime.** In the MuseumsQuartier: *Death and Life* and several of his square landscapes of the Attersee, the lake where he spent summers.
4. **Upper Belvedere, afternoon.** *The Kiss*, *Judith I* and the late, unfinished *Adam and Eve* and *Portrait of Amalie Zuckerkandl*, where you can see how he built a painting.

With more time, the **Wien Museum** (free permanent collection) has *Pallas Athene* and his portrait of **Emilie Flöge**, his lifelong companion. The **Klimt Villa** in Hietzing is his last studio, restored.

## The Woman in Gold

The story behind the Neue Galerie’s star painting is one of the best-known restitution cases in art. *Portrait of Adele Bloch-Bauer I* was seized by the Nazis after Austria’s annexation in 1938 and later hung in the Belvedere as a national treasure. Adele’s niece, Maria Altmann, who had fled to the United States, fought the Austrian government for years. The case went to the US Supreme Court, and in 2006 an arbitration panel in Austria returned five Klimts to her family. Ronald Lauder bought the portrait the same year for the Neue Galerie, where it has hung ever since. The story became the 2015 film *Woman in Gold*.

## The Klimts you can’t see

**Burned.** In 1945 retreating SS troops set fire to Schloss Immendorf in Lower Austria, where several Klimts had been stored for safety. Among the losses were his three **Faculty Paintings** (*Philosophy*, *Medicine* and *Jurisprudence*) for the University of Vienna, whose nudity had caused a scandal. They survive only in photographs, almost all of them black and white.

**Private.** Many of the great portraits are privately owned. In November 2025 the *Portrait of Elisabeth Lederer* sold at Sotheby’s in New York for about $236 million, a record for Klimt and for any modern artwork at auction. The previous Klimt record, *Lady with a Fan*, had been set in 2023. Neither is on public view.

**Stolen and found.** *Portrait of a Lady* disappeared from the Galleria Ricci Oddi in Piacenza in 1997. In 2019, gardeners clearing ivy found it in a small cavity in the gallery’s own outer wall. It’s back on display.

## Before you go

- **Book the Belvedere for opening time.** *The Kiss* gets crowded by late morning.
- **Klimt in gold is hard to photograph.** The gold leaf glares under gallery lights. Stand slightly off to one side.
- **Check exhibitions.** Vienna’s museums lend and borrow Klimts for exhibitions, and many smaller works on paper are shown only in rotation.
